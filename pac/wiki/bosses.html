👹 Bosses

DRAFT (assistant) This page summarizes BOSS-FIGHTS.md, which is itself a DRAFT design built on owner canon — fight structures, phases, numbers and matchups are proposals until the owner rules. Canon facts are marked CANON. Only two fights are designed in that file so far (the Ape, Batman); the rest of the boss roster is TBD.

The boss doctrine

  • CANON A win = killing the level boss. Bosses gate progress absolutely — no kill, no next level, no unlock pool from that level.
  • CANON Bosses spawn on math, not clocks — when your build's power rating crosses his threshold.
  • CANON Refusing backfires: a refused boss becomes a spawner, and his escalation timer runs amplified. Refusing borrows time at interest.
  • CANON Fights are epic and long — multi-phase set pieces in minutes; patterns escalate, the arena changes, builds must adapt.
  • CANON Mechanic-to-win law: every boss has a mechanic you need to follow to win. Raw DPS never substitutes.
  • CANON Raid kit: each fight draws from LOS · AOE telegraphs · aggro/threat · mobs/adds · puzzles · danger zones · environmental debuffs — no fight uses all seven.
  • CANON Matchups exist by design — certain characters are best for certain bosses, and every boss answers some builds back.
  • CANON Pre-boss fairness: offers on the road to a boss are seeded so you already hold at least 3 good combat spells that work on him.
  • CANON Comic-intro note: every boss gets a character introduction in comic windows (notebook-paper panels, gutters, narration boxes) before the fight — you meet the boss before you fight him. The intro stagings in BOSS-FIGHTS.md are DRAFT staging of canon beats.

1. The Ape — first mini-boss, Level 6 DRAFT fight design

CANON A giant ape in the Donkey Kong mould who throws barrels that explode. He waits at Level 6 — the gate where you've just completed Bank (L4) · Weapon (L5) · Stone (L6). You do not face him unarmed, unbanked, or unstoned. (His playable racer build exists separately — two chairs, one ape.)

Mechanic-to-win (DRAFT): Bait the throw, break the scaffold. Your damage can't reach him on his girder platform — his exploding barrels can. Stand where a throw will land beside a cracked scaffold pylon, be gone when it lands, and let his own explosives demolish his perch — four pylons, then he fights you on the ground, where he is finally edible.

Phases (DRAFT): 1) The Throwing Lesson — single snapshot-telegraphed barrels, two blasts break a pylon. 2) The Stockpile Gets Serious — double throws, red barrels, barrel-ape adds, platform tilts at the third pylon. 3) Demolition — he protects the last pylon; Chest Pound punishes lingering; the Banked power is the intended answer. 4) On The Ground — he lands enraged and takes full damage.

Raid kit (DRAFT): AOE telegraphs and puzzles core; LOS and aggro light (a pet near a pylon is a legal bait tool); adds from Phase 2; danger zones yes; arena debuff deliberately none — first boss, one lesson at a time.

Refusal (DRAFT form of canon law): refuse him and exploding barrels keep rolling into the level's corridors on his amplified timer. Matchups (DRAFT): answered by high-mobility, Teleport/Time Shift, pet-tank and Bank-heavy builds; he answers stationary turret/channel builds and slow tanks in Phase 4.

2. Batman — the adaptive boss DRAFT fight design

CANON Batman studies your build between waves and returns countering it — resistant to your main element, ready for your favourite trick. He reads the opposition chart before he reads anything else. He preps in silence, which Pac finds rude. (A playable Batman build exists separately — this is the boss.)

Mechanic-to-win (DRAFT): Never show him the same fight twice. After every phase he preps against the damage source, element and trick you used most and becomes near-immune to it, with a gadget that punishes it. You win by rotating — each phase must be won with a different primary tool. Your Bank, materia swaps, second weapon line and pet are the fight.

Phases (DRAFT): a repeating study cycle — Phase A (the Examination, fought against his prepped counter-loadout) → the Study Break (he grapples to a gargoyle, untouchable; comic panels read back what he learned; your free re-tool window) → further cycles where his prep stacks — until the final cycle, where the kill must be a tool he has never seen: a banked never-fired power, an unspent stone alteration, or a freshly re-socketed element.

Raid kit (DRAFT): LOS and puzzles core (keep him in sight or his next attack is an ambush); AOE, aggro (pet taunts burn his one Answer per cycle), danger zones and an arena debuff (his study itself) yes; adds light — installed gadgets, not swarm mobs.

Refusal (DRAFT form): refuse Batman and he preps longer — the worst refusal in the game, by design. Matchups (DRAFT): answered by hybrid/triadic palettes, Bank-maximal builds, materia swappers, pet and Theft builds; he answers mono-element builds, single-Mythic carries, dash-spam, and summon one-shots.

3. Bomberguy — the main villain CANON

Black Bomberman DNA — round white bomb head, antenna, huge grin; the Sith layer is cape + black armour. His weapon is area denial in his own law: planted bomb grids whose blasts run in cross/corridor lines (Bomberman rules), carving the arena into shrinking safe zones. Encore rule: after every level boss dies, Bomberguy appears for an encore fight and always gets away (bomb-smoke escape) — until the final level, where he doesn't run. Every escape ends with an original curse, never repeated, logged in the Curse Ledger; Pac answers foully. Reveal: he is Darth Bomberguy, sent by the Empire because World 1's boss is the Emperor — whom Pac eats. A full Bomberguy boss-fight design is TBD (not in BOSS-FIGHTS.md yet).

4. The Emperor — World 1 CANON

World 1's boss is the Emperor — whom Pac eats. Disposable by design: he dies, no design investment. His cackled “DO IT” in the intro is the whole investment he gets.

Enemies are EVIL CANON tone law

Enemies are evil — including hero characters appearing as bosses, and characters who later unlock as good. Their enemy versions are written and drawn as villains: villain intros, villain lines, villain spoof art. Unlocking flips the playable copy to the hero version; the enemy version stays evil forever and can reappear exactly as villainous. The adaptive Batman is the standing example: as a boss he is a menace; the playable Batman is a separate copy.

Bomberguy keeps catching you off guard CANON

Bomberguy is the bad guy, and ambush is his pattern — beyond the after-boss encore (bomb-smoke escape, an original curse for the ledger, every time): mid-level surprise appearances where a bomb grid snaps in around you with no radar warning, and story ambushes where he crashes other bosses' comic-window intros and is gone by the next panel. He escalates across the worlds and stops running only at the final level. DRAFT structure Frequencies, fuse timings and fairness rails are assistant DRAFT in BOSS-FIGHTS.md, Appendix B.
