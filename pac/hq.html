🍕 PAC — JUDGEMENT DAY

“It's a pop culture roguelike survivor MOBA.”

About the game. You are Pac — the real one, give or take a spoof — dropped into a huge lane map split corner to corner. Your little units march the lanes beside you; the enemy swarm marches the other way. Eat everything that isn't nailed down — pellets laid out in highways and patterns, fruit, coins, rings, and once in a while an Infinity Stone — grow bigger, hit harder, and feed your towers until three of theirs fall. Then the lair opens: micro-towers, puzzles, and the boss in his chamber. Beat him and he joins your roster — every enemy in this game is a hero you haven't unlocked yet, and every hero is somebody you recognize, pencil-drawn on notebook paper. Runs are roguelike: what you unlock, craft and discover stays yours. What you can't do is stand still — the dots grow where you're losing and dry up where you're comfortable, and the swarm does not stop coming. Neither do you.

📜 Rules of the Desk

  1. Piece by piece, step by step. Models → animations → systems as data → UI → the map alone. One step is built, shown, and approved before the next starts. No one-shot builds.
  2. The wiki is the game, written first. The whole game exists as written pages before code follows it. If it isn't in the wiki, it isn't in the game.
  3. Math → data → build. The math and the data tables for a system land before anything is built on it (sequencing law).
  4. The flowchart test. You should be able to tell in a flowchart if the game is broken. If a break isn't visible on paper, the paper is incomplete — fix the paper first.
  5. No fan art. Anything shown to Brent must be replicable in the real game — engine output, working code, or buildable data/specs. Concept art is never presented as progress.
  6. Peer review — all ideas cross-checked. Every substantive delivery gets a second AI's eyes before it counts — including Kanye's own proposals, which Claude cold-reads.
  7. Two-reviewer gate on Carlon. Nothing from Carlon counts until Kanye has reviewed it AND Claude has cross-reviewed it.
  8. Clear-approach rule. A question or decision for Brent is never buried inside a file. It arrives as its own flagged item — here, and later as a needs-brent ticket.

  10. Canon discipline. Only Brent's words are canon. Everything else is labelled DRAFT and stays a proposal until he rules. Workers never edit canon files.
  11. Model matching. Every assignment names the worker AND the model/mode, with the reason: deep-reasoning models for audits, architecture and math; fast models for volume and formatting.
